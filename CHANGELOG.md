# Everything WoW Companion Changelog

Each entry names the version its tag carries. The same text stands under Changes in the README.

**0.3.2** gives the Companion a window. `/ewow` opens it, and so does a new button on the minimap. Until now everything the addon did was a chat command; every command still works.

- **The Route page.** Two boxes, From and To, a speed button and Plan Route. Leave From empty and the trip starts where your character stands. The answer is the planner's own, the one `/ewow route` prints: the trip, the estimate and the steps in order, eight to a page. A click on the speed button goes round the four speeds and plans the trip again. WoW Forever only, like the planner; on any other client the window opens on the Recorded page.
- **The Recorded page.** What the file holds now, kind by kind, in the words the site's upload page uses, how many observations were dropped to keep the file small, where the file is written and where to upload it. On every client.
- **Deals.** A third button opens the list of auctions under vendor price, which keeps its own window.
- **The minimap button.** A click opens or closes the window, and a drag moves the button round the minimap's edge. `/ewow minimap` hides it or shows it again. Whether it is hidden and where it sits are the two things the window saves.
- **The commands.** `/ewow` alone opens the window, where it used to print the status. `/ewow status` prints the status as before, and `/ewow route` with no place opens the Route page. On a client where the window cannot be built, `/ewow` prints the status as it always did.
- **Nothing new is read.** The window shows what the commands show, and it records nothing.
- **The map's facts of 24 November.** `TravelData.lua` is written again from the site's files. Farholde Keep flies to Lakeshire, Morgan's Vigil and Thelsamar, and Rog'mar to Stonard, Hammerfall, Kargath and Flame Crest; Powderfuse Port and Tidegear Coast have no flight master yet; and the Alliance can walk the beach north of Theramore to Ratchet, with a warning about the murlocs that hold it. Every answer was compared again with the site's planner, on 9,680 trips, and the tests now keep 263 of them.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `Window.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.3.2`. The tests open the window on a stand in for the client's frames, type a trip, page through it, change the speed, read the Recorded page, click and drag the minimap button, and load a client that cannot build the window; the suite reads 2,759 assertions, from 2,635, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.3.1** times the flights you take. The site's map knows how long 195 Classic flights take, measured by players long ago, and can only guess at every flight master new in WoW Forever. Now each flight you take from end to end is written down, so the guesses can be replaced by what the flight really took.

- **`Flights.lua`.** One flight is one observation of the kind `flight`: where you stood when you opened the flight master's map, the two flight points as the game's flight map names them, the seconds from takeoff to landing to a tenth, the faction whose network it is, and where you landed. Nothing else of the player is in it.
- **Only whole flights.** A flight is written only when it was seen from the pick on the flight map to the landing. One ended early with the request to land, one a loading screen cut, anything under five seconds or over half an hour, and a loss of control the client never called a taxi are left out.
- **No new request.** The recorder reads what the flight map already shows and listens for the takeoff and the landing. Where the client offers no way to learn the destination picked, it registers nothing and writes nothing.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `Flights.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.3.1`. The tests fly a plain flight, one with a stop on the way, and each kind that must not be written, and the site's own reader accepts a file holding a flight; the suite reads 2,635 assertions, from 2,596, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.3.0** brings everythingwow.com's route planner into the game. `/ewow route Booty Bay` plans the trip from where your character stands, and `/ewow route Orgrimmar > Undercity` plans it between any two places: the steps in order, and how long the trip takes as a low, a usual and a high time. It is the first part of the travel features; the route on the world map comes next.

- **The site's own planner.** `Travel.lua` is the planner behind the site's WoW Forever map, written again in Lua, and `TravelData.lua` is the site's own map data: 46 zones, 127 places, 49 land crossings, 15 boats, zeppelins, trams and portals, and 195 measured flight times. `tools/gen_travel_data.py` writes that file from the site's files, so the game and the website answer the same trip the same way.
- **What it reads.** Your faction, whether your character is a Skyborne, and the map you stand on. Nothing else, and it sends nothing. It records nothing: a planned route makes no observation.
- **`/ewow route speed`.** Routes are timed on foot until you say otherwise: `/ewow route speed 60`, `100`, `travel` or `foot`. That one setting is the only thing the planner saves.
- **WoW Forever only.** The facts are that world's, so on any other client the command says so and stops.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `TravelData.lua` and `Travel.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.3.0`. Every answer was compared with the site's planner on 7,852 trips before release, and the tests keep 258 of them, each compared step by step, to the second and to the word, beside the name search, the map lookup and the command; the suite reads 2,596 assertions, from 842, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.13** leaves alone the items the site already has. On 24 November 2026 the owner's first file from 0.2.12 held 400 item observations, the session's whole allowance, and 340 of them were items the site already had from Blizzard: an id of 200,000 or more turned out to be a weak sign of a missing item, since Blizzard's own WoW Forever data holds 11,770 such ids.

- **`Known.lua`.** A new file holds those 11,770 ids as 2,147 ranges, as the site's public database held them on 24 November 2026. It is data, not code, and nothing in it is read from the player's game.
- **The recorder.** `Items.lua` no longer notes an item on that list, and never asks the item cache about one. Everything else is as in 0.2.12: WoW Forever alone, ids from 200,000 up, once per item per fourteen days, 400 a session.
- **A stale list is harmless.** A listed item is one the site has. An item Blizzard adds later is noted and the site leaves it alone, since it adds a page only where it holds none. A list that cannot be read is treated as no list, and every item is noted as before.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `Known.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.2.13`. The tests read the shipped list (its order, its 2,147 ranges and 11,770 ids), a listed and an unlisted item, the ends of a range, a single id, the gaps, and five lists that cannot be read; the suite reads 842 assertions, from 806, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.12** notes the items Blizzard's item API does not hold. On 23 November 2026 the site's worker asked the API by id for four items WoW Forever's own auction houses sell, and each answered 404; 295 of the 296 auction items the site had no page for were new WoW Forever items, ids above 200,000. Their names and what they are can only come from players.

- **A new kind, `item`.** The first time the addon meets such an item, at the auction house, in loot, at a vendor or in a quest's rewards, it writes one observation of it from the client's own item cache: `n`, the name; `ql`, the quality number; `il`, the item level; `rl`, the level it requires; `c` and `sc`, the class and subclass numbers; `slot`, the equip location token; `stack`; `sell`, what a vendor pays in copper; `bind`; `stats`, the base item's stats by the client's own keys; and `sp`, the spell a use triggers.
- **The same for every player.** The item is asked for by its bare id, never by the link in front of the player, so a random suffix, an enchant or a maker's name never reaches the file. Nothing of the player is in the observation and no position is written.
- **WoW Forever alone, and once.** It runs only on WoW Forever and only for ids from 200,000 up, where the gap is. The saved file remembers the day each item was noted (`items`, beside `observations`) and notes it again only after fourteen days, after a patch or after `/ewow clear`. A session writes at most 400, so a first walk through a full auction house never pushes the NPCs, loot and quests out of the buffer.
- **No new event and no query.** The recorders that already meet items hand them over. An item the client has not loaded yet is tried again the next few times an item is met, the way the deals window reads a sell price, and then left for another session.
- **Before the site reads it.** The site's reader drops a kind it does not know and counts it, and reads the rest of the file as before, so this version is safe to run ahead of the site.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `Items.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.2.12`. The tests note an item from each of the four places, a stacking item with a use, a name holding a client code, an item the client has not loaded, the session cap, the fortnight, a patch and a clear, and prove every other client writes nothing; the Forever file they write carries an item observation and the saved memory, which the site's own parser reads. The suite reads 806 assertions, from 726, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.11** records what the quest window says. On 23 November 2026 the owner asked for a quest's text, objectives and rewards: Blizzard's WoW Forever quest index holds a small part of the game's quests, and the pages the site makes from this addon's quest titles carried a name and a quest giver and nothing else.

- **At the offer.** A `quest_start` observation now carries `text`, the quest's description, and `objectives`, what the player is asked to do.
- **At the turn in.** A `quest_end` observation carries `completion`, what the NPC says.
- **At both, where the window shows them.** `rewards` and `choices`, each a list of `{ id, q, n }`, `n` being the item's name as the window shows it, so the site can name a reward Blizzard's item index does not hold; `money` in copper; `xp`; and `level`, the quest's level, where the client can give it.
- **The player is never recorded.** The game writes the character's own name into quest text, so every text is scrubbed before it is stored: the name, whole and word by word, becomes the token `<name>`. The client's color codes are removed, and a text still holding a link or a control character other than a line break is not recorded at all. Class and race words are left as the game wrote them.
- **Sizes.** The description is kept to 1,600 bytes, the objectives to 600 and the completion text to 1,200, each cut on a character boundary, and the whole payload is then held under the reader's 4,000 byte cap by shortening the longest text first and marking it `cut`, so a long quest is recorded shorter rather than dropped.
- **No new event and no new call outside the quest window.** The two events are the ones the recorder has used since its first release. Every function is one the quest window itself is drawn from, each inside `pcall`, and a client without one records the quest as before.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.11`. The tests read an offer and a turn in with rewards and choices, a two word name, a link, a control character, a cut inside a character and a quest whose text swells in JSON, and the Forever file they write now carries a quest's text, which the site's own parser reads; the suite reads 726 assertions, from 678, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4. The site does not show these fields yet.

**0.2.10** reads a grouped item row as the price of one. On 23 November 2026 the owner opened Blue Leather Bag from the new window and the row changed to "Buy 64 for 62s 32c": the auction house showed 15 bags at 8 silver 67 each, and the addon had taken 8 silver 67 for all 15.

- **The cause.** The modern auction house groups identical auctions of an item into one row, and `GetItemSearchResultInfo` gives that row's `buyoutAmount` as the price of one and `quantity` as how many there are at that price. Both readers took `buyoutAmount` for the whole row.
- **The window.** An item search now counts a row as `quantity` at `buyoutAmount` each, so the bags read as what they are: a few copper under the vendor's price each, and only the rows priced under it.
- **The recorder.** The same read in `Auction.lua` wrote such a row with `unit` as the price divided by the quantity. It now writes `unit` as the price of one and `buyout` as the price of the row, as the browse and commodity reads always have. Rows of one, which is most gear, were always right. The site publishes no price from these rows (its Forever prices come from Blizzard's auction data through the worker), so nothing shown on the site was wrong.
- **`/ewow deals min <amount>`.** Hides deals with less profit than the amount, for the session: `/ewow deals min 50s`, `/ewow deals min 1g`, and `/ewow deals min 0` to show every deal again.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.10`. The tests read the owner's own row of bags through both readers and fail against 0.2.9; the suite reads 678 assertions, from 656, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.9** adds a window of auctions listed under their vendor price. On 23 November 2026 the owner asked for the addon to look for items being sold for less than a vendor pays for them, and to show what to buy and for how much, from the same auction house reading the recorder already does.

- **What it finds.** An auction is a deal when its buyout for one item is lower than the item's vendor sell price, so buying it and selling it to any vendor leaves a profit. An item no vendor buys is never a deal, and neither is one of the player's own auctions. The sell price is the client's own, from its item cache; an item the client has not loaded yet is asked again a second later, up to three times.
- **How it reads.** Exactly as the recorder does: only the results the player's own search has already loaded, on the same events and through the same getters, never in combat. It sends no query, buys nothing and bids on nothing. A commodity search or an item search gives every listing, so its row is exact: how many to buy, what they cost, what a vendor pays and the profit. The browse list gives only each item's lowest price, so its row is the price of one, marked `1+`, until the player opens the item. On a client without `C_AuctionHouse` it reads each page of the classic list. It runs where the recorder reads the auction house, which is Forever and the classic clients, and is idle on Retail and on a client the addon cannot place.
- **The window.** It opens by itself beside the auction house the first time a visit finds a deal, lists the deals with the most profit first, twelve to a page, and closes with the auction house or with Escape. A row shows the item's tooltip under the pointer, and a click hands the item's link to the client the way a shift click does. Every call that builds it sits inside `pcall`, and on a client where it cannot be built the list is printed in chat instead. A new visit to the auction house starts a new list.
- **`/ewow deals`.** Opens or closes the window. `/ewow deals list` prints the list in chat, `/ewow deals clear` empties it, and `/ewow deals auto off` keeps the window closed until asked for, for the session, with one line of chat when a deal is found.
- **Nothing is uploaded.** A deal is never written to the saved file: `EverythingWoWDB` is unchanged, and so is everything the site reads.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents list `Deals.lua` and carry `## Version: 0.2.9`. The tests drive the finder through a stand in `C_AuctionHouse` and a stand in item cache on Forever, Retail and Classic Era, prove no query is ever sent and nothing reaches the saved file, and build the window against a stand in frame; the suite reads 656 assertions, from 573, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4. The window itself has not been seen in a game client.

**0.2.8** records what kind of creature an NPC is and the title under its name. On 12 November 2026 the owner asked that the site's pages for NPCs the Companion has named say more than a name: the level and whether a creature is elite or rare were already in every sighting, and the type and the title were not.

- **The creature type.** A sighting now carries `creature_type`, the client's own word for the kind of creature, "Beast" or "Humanoid", read from the unit itself wherever a sighting is made. A client that names none writes none.
- **The title.** A sighting carries `title`, the line a creature shows under its name, "Innkeeper" or "Flight Master". No unit function returns it, so it is read from the second line of the creature's own tooltip, and only while the tooltip is on that creature: a nameplate sighting made while the pointer is elsewhere carries no title. The level line, which sits second on a creature with no title, is told apart by the client's own word for level. A line with a color code or a control character, or longer than 64 characters, is not recorded. A player, a pet and a vehicle are passed over before either read, as they always were.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.8`. The tests read a titled innkeeper, a boar whose second line is its level line, a nameplate sighting under another unit's tooltip, a painted line, an over long line and no tooltip; the suite reads 573 assertions, from 563, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.7** keeps the saved file small enough to upload. On 8 November 2026 the owner's file passed 8 MB, the most the site's upload page reads, and the page refused it. The buffer was held to 5,000 observations but not to a size, and Forever's auction pages, which 0.2.6 began reading from the player's own searches, run to about 1.5 KB of JSON each, about 2.6 KB in the saved file: in the owner's 6 November file 727 auction pages took 1.9 MB of its 2.9 MB, and a buffer that fills with them passes 8 MB well before 5,000.

- **Two byte budgets beside the 5,000.** The buffer is now also held to 3,600,000 bytes of JSON, measured the way the upload page measures what it sends, which is about 6 MB on disk and sits under the 3,900,000 byte body the page sends in one upload, so every observation in the file goes up together. Inside that, auction pages have a budget of their own, 1,200,000 bytes, about 800 pages, and past it the oldest auction page is dropped rather than the oldest observation, so a long session at the auction house never pushes out NPCs, loot, quests, or vendors. Every observation either budget lets go is counted in `dropped`, as the 5,000 always has been, and the newest observation is never dropped.
- **A file already over budget shrinks.** The budgets are applied once when the addon loads as well as after every write, so a file 0.2.6 wrote past 8 MB is cut back at the next login, oldest auction pages first, and the game writes the smaller file at the next logout or `/reload`.
- **The version and the tests.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.7`. The tests fill the buffer with auction pages past their budget beside 50 NPCs and prove every NPC is kept, fill it with vendors past the file budget, prove the running totals match a fresh measure, and load a 4,000 page file written before 0.2.7 and prove it is pruned to both budgets; the suite reads 563 assertions, from 548, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4. A 6,000 observation Forever session, a third of it auction pages, writes a 2.9 MB file that the site's own parser reads whole.

**0.2.6** records each class's talent trees for the site's talent calculators. On 20 October 2026 the owner decided the Classic three tree calculator's data is recorded by the Companion from the game client's own talent functions: Forever first, on its launch day, 4 November 2026, with a fresh character of each of the nine classes, and Classic Era later. Blizzard's Classic Game Data API publishes no talent document, and a datamined table or another site's database is not a source the site uses, so the client is the only source.

It also makes the addon silent at login and reads Forever's auction house. On 24 October 2026 the owner asked that the chat line 0.2.2 printed in Forever, "Auction recording is off for this client; the auction listener stays idle.", go away, and that the addon upload as much data as it can.

- **Silent idle recorders.** No recorder prints anything at login any more. The idle auction line 0.2.2 added was the only one; it is gone, and `/ewow status` now names the recorders that cannot run on the client instead, on an `idle on this client:` line, for example "kill counter (combatLog off)" on Forever or "auction recording (the site reads Blizzard's auction API)" on Retail. Every command's own output, `/ewow status`, `/ewow probe`, `/ewow talents` and the rest, is unchanged, and so is the line a forbidden action report prints.
- **Forever's auction house, read from your own searches.** Forever's client carries the Retail project id, so its auction house is the modern `C_AuctionHouse` one, which the classic `AUCTION_ITEM_LIST_UPDATE` scan cannot read, and Blizzard publishes no auction API for Forever's realms. A new reader in Auction.lua reads what your own searches have already loaded: the browse list (`AUCTION_HOUSE_BROWSE_RESULTS_UPDATED` and `_ADDED`, through `C_AuctionHouse.GetBrowseResults`: each row's item, lowest price and total quantity), a commodity search (`COMMODITY_SEARCH_RESULTS_UPDATED` and `_ADDED`: each listing's unit price and quantity) and an item search (`ITEM_SEARCH_RESULTS_UPDATED` and `_ADDED`: each auction's buyout and quantity). It never sends a query of its own, never reads a seller, an owner list or a bidder, and writes the classic scan's own shape, `{ items = { { id, q, unit, buyout } } }` in copper, 50 rows to an observation and at most 500 rows for one announcement, with a `source` field naming which of the three reads wrote it, since a browse row's price is the lowest of every listing of the item. A search repeated within a minute reads only the rows the client added.
- **A new capability, `auctionModern`.** On for Forever and every classic row, off for Retail, whose auctions the site reads from Blizzard's own API, and for an unknown client. The reader registers its events only the first time you open the auction house out of combat, never at login, and only the ones the client says exist (`C_EventUtils.IsEventValid`) and whose getters it carries, so a client without `C_AuctionHouse` registers nothing at all. Every call sits inside `pcall`, nothing runs in combat, and a refusal of any of its events turns off `auctionModern` alone. The same reader serves Cataclysm Classic and Mists Classic wherever they carry `C_AuctionHouse`, and the classic scan now steps aside only where the browse list can actually be read. `/ewow probe` asks about the reader's seven events too, nineteen in all.

- **`/ewow talents`.** Reads the current character's class trees and keeps them: the class file token, the site's class slug and the class id, the client key, `WOW_PROJECT_ID`, the build, build number and interface number from `GetBuildInfo`, the character's level, a timestamp and the addon version, then every tree (id where the client gives one, index, name, icon, background) and every talent (id where the client gives one, name, icon, tier, column, maximum rank, the milestone mark, and each prerequisite by tier and column with the prerequisite's id and index where it resolves). It prints, for example, "Recorded 3 trees, 51 talents for Warrior. /reload or log out to save." and the classes held so far for the client. Nothing about the character beyond its class and level is written: no name, no realm, and no rank the character has spent.
- **Both talent APIs, behind feature detection.** Forever's client carries the Retail project id, so its talents may come through the classic functions (`GetNumTalentTabs`, `GetTalentTabInfo`, `GetNumTalents`, `GetTalentInfo`, `GetTalentPrereqs`, and `GetTalentLink` for the id) or through the modern ones (`C_ClassTalents.GetActiveConfigID` and `C_Traits`' config, tree, node, entry and definition info). Both are read, every call inside `pcall`, the classic shape first. A trait node's tier and column are its rank among its tree's heights and widths, with the raw position kept beside them, and its prerequisites are the nodes whose arrows point at it. Where both answer, the second is kept beside the first under `alt`. Where neither answers, the capture is a diagnostic naming which of the functions the client has and why each path gave nothing, and a level one character is told to try again at level 10. A failed read never replaces a good capture of the same class. The command waits for the player to leave combat.
- **Its own saved variable.** The captures live in `EverythingWoWTalentsDB`, declared beside `EverythingWoWDB` in both tables of contents and written into the same `EverythingWoW.lua`, one per class per client under a `<client>:<CLASS>` key, so a second read of a class replaces the first and nine classes give nine entries. The site's upload page reads `EverythingWoWDB` alone and sends only its version, patch, addon and observations, so the captures never reach the upload's validation, its observation count or its payload caps, and an upload of the file goes through exactly as before. The site does not read the captures yet, so on launch day the file goes to the Orchestrator. `/ewow clear` leaves the captures alone, and `/ewow talents clear` throws them away.
- **The version, the path line and the tests.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.6` and keep the `## Interface` lists 0.2.5 wrote, `120100, 50504, 20506, 16001, 11509` and `11509`. `/ewow path` now prints the game folder, `_<flavor>_`, in the path. The test stand in's `GetBuildInfo` reads `unpack` where `table.unpack` is missing, so the suite runs under Lua 5.1, the game's own Lua, as well as 5.4. The tests read a three tree classic shape with an arrow, a `C_Traits` shape with an edge, a choice node and an empty node, both at once, and neither, and the Retail and Forever files they write carry `EverythingWoWTalentsDB` beside `EverythingWoWDB`, the Forever one with all nine classes, so the worker's file check proves the site's parser still accepts a file holding both. The tests also drive the modern auction reader through a stand in `C_AuctionHouse` on Forever, Retail, Cataclysm Classic and Classic Era, and the Forever file now carries a browse page and a commodity search; the suite reads 548 assertions, from 459, under Lua 5.1 and 5.4.

**0.2.5** keys a Wrath of the Lich King Classic or Cataclysm Classic session as the site's own rows without declaring either client to the stores. On 28 September 2026 the site's `versions` table gained two rows, `classic_wrath` and `classic_cata`, both left disabled, and the site's upload path reads every row whether or not it is enabled, so an upload keyed to either is accepted while no page of the site shows either dataset.

- **Wrath of the Lich King Classic and Cataclysm Classic read as themselves.** Once the Forever and Retail checks have passed, `EW.ReadClient` now keys a session of version major 3 as `classic_wrath` and one of major 4 as `classic_cata`, beside majors 2 and 5, and a readable major of 6 or above off the mainline project id still reads `unknown`. `EW.VersionKey` answers with the client key outright for both, as it does for `forever`, `classic_tbc`, and `classic_mop`, so `db.version` and `db.client` agree for both. `/ewow status` prints the new keys through the same `game` and `client` lines it has printed since 0.2.1. Under 0.2.4 a 3.x or 4.x session was keyed `unknown`, a key the site refuses, so its upload was turned away.
- **Two new capability profiles.** `classic_wrath` and `classic_cata` carry Classic Era's row: `worldCursor` off, and `unitGuid`, `combatLog`, and `auction` on. Auction.lua's own scan steps aside wherever `C_AuctionHouse` exists, so the row holds whichever auction window each client carries. No other Lua file reads the client key, so none of them changed.
- **No new client declared.** Both tables of contents carry `## Version: 0.2.5` and keep the `## Interface` lists 0.2.4 wrote, `120100, 50504, 20506, 16001, 11509` and `11509`, so the stores offer this release for the same five clients. Neither Wrath of the Lich King Classic nor Cataclysm Classic can be played in a region the site reads, `us`, `eu`, `kr`, or `tw`: the only 3.x client still running is Titan Reforged, a China only Classic+ client at 3.80.2, and Cataclysm Classic's realms moved to the Mists of Pandaria pre-patch on 1 July 2025. Declaring either client would offer a download no player in those regions can use.

**0.2.4** declares every client the site holds and keys their uploads correctly. The site's `versions` table held exactly five enabled rows when read on 27 September 2026, `retail`, `classic_era`, `classic_tbc`, `classic_mop`, and `forever`, but 0.2.3's tables of contents carried only the Retail and Classic Era interface numbers, so the stores offered that release for those two clients alone. The README's Wago link also moves to the project's renamed address, <https://addons.wago.io/addons/everything-wow-companion>, the id `kGr9yAKy` being unchanged.

- **Burning Crusade Classic and Mists Classic read as themselves.** Once the Forever and Retail checks have passed, `EW.ReadClient` now keys a session by its version string's major: 2 reads `classic_tbc` (Burning Crusade Classic Anniversary, 2.5.x), 5 reads `classic_mop` (Mists of Pandaria Classic, 5.5.x), 1 below Forever's minimum reads `classic_era` or `hardcore` as before, and any other readable major off the mainline project id, such as Wrath of the Lich King or Cataclysm Classic, reads `unknown` and takes the most restrictive capability row. Under 0.2.3 a Burning Crusade or Mists session fell through to the Classic Era branch, was keyed `classic_era`, and wrote `classic_era` into `db.version`, so its upload would have landed in the Classic Era database under the wrong key.
- **The upload's key follows the client's.** `EW.VersionKey` answers with the client key outright for `forever`, `classic_tbc`, `classic_mop`, and `unknown`, so `db.version` and `db.client` cannot disagree for any of the four, and otherwise answers as it always has: `retail` for the mainline project id, and `hardcore` or `classic_era` for the rest. An `unknown` key is one the site's upload page refuses, since its `versions` table holds no such row, where 0.2.3 sent `classic_era` for the same session. `/ewow status` prints the new keys through the same `game` and `client` lines it has printed since 0.2.1.
- **Two new capability profiles.** `classic_tbc` and `classic_mop` carry Classic Era's row: `worldCursor` off, and `unitGuid`, `combatLog`, and `auction` on. Neither client has `C_TooltipInfo.GetWorldCursor`, and both keep the combat log. Auction.lua's own scan runs only where `C_AuctionHouse` is absent and steps aside where it exists, as it does on Retail, so the row holds whichever auction window each client carries. No other Lua file reads the client key, so none of them changed.
- **Five clients declared.** `EverythingWoW.toc` now carries `## Interface: 120100, 50504, 20506, 16001, 11509`, for Retail 12.1.0, Mists Classic 5.5.4, Burning Crusade Anniversary 2.5.6, Forever 1.60.1, and Classic Era 1.15.9, and the packager reads that list as five game versions. `EverythingWoW_Vanilla.toc` keeps `## Interface: 11509`. Wrath of the Lich King and Cataclysm are not declared, because the site holds no version rows for them and would refuse their uploads.

**0.2.3** sends the `forever` version key for a Forever session. The site's `versions` table has had its `forever` row enabled since 24 September 2026, so the asymmetry 0.2.1 documented is closed.

- **Forever recordings land under Forever.** `EW.VersionKey`, the key written to `db.version`, now answers `forever` whenever `EW.Client` reads as `forever`, that is, a version string of major 1 and minor 60 or above, whatever project id the client carries. The upload's own field therefore reads `forever` for the owner's build 1.60.1 session, and its recordings land in the Forever database rather than Retail's. Retail, Classic Era, Hardcore, and a client the table cannot place answer exactly as they did in 0.2.2.
- **`db.client` still rides beside it.** It carries the client this addon detected, now the same key as `db.version` for a Forever session, and `/ewow status`'s `game` line still reads it. Nothing else changed: the capability table, the probe, the forbidden handler, and every recorder are exactly as 0.2.2 shipped them.

**0.2.2** settles the Forever capability profile on the owner's own `/ewow probe` transcript, run against 0.2.1 on build 1.60.1: `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_ADDED`, `UPDATE_MOUSEOVER_UNIT`, and `PLAYER_TARGET_CHANGED` came back allowed, `COMBAT_LOG_EVENT_UNFILTERED` and `AUCTION_ITEM_LIST_UPDATE` came back refused, and the owner's own `/ewow cap worldCursor on` and `/ewow cap unitGuid on` afterward raised no alert. The same transcript also showed a second alert the probe itself caused: `ADDON_ACTION_FORBIDDEN` reported for `EverythingWoWFrame:UnregisterEvent()`, which turned every restricted capability off, worldCursor and unitGuid included, on the strength of a refusal 0.2.1 could not place.

- **The Forever profile.** `worldCursor` and `unitGuid` now default on for Forever, `combatLog` stays off, and a new `auction` capability, gating `AUCTION_ITEM_LIST_UPDATE` and this addon's own auction API calls, defaults off too. Retail's `auction` capability is on, and Classic Era and Hardcore keep their long standing unrestricted behavior, where Auction.lua's own scan is the only way the auction house gets recorded at all.
- **A refused registration is never followed by an unregister call.** The probe used to call `UnregisterEvent` unconditionally after every attempt, including a refused one, but a refused registration was never actually registered, so there was nothing to unregister, and the owner's own build 1.60.1 session proved that calling it anyway is itself forbidden. The probe now skips that call entirely when the registration was refused.
- **An UnregisterEvent refusal is attributed to the exact event, the same as a RegisterEvent refusal.** Two bugs combined to make 0.2.1's fallback the wrong one: `EW.lastRegisterAttempt` was already cleared by the time the unconditional unregister call ran, and `NamesRegisterEvent`'s substring check for `"RegisterEvent"` does not match `"UnregisterEvent"`, which spells its own `register` with a lowercase `r`. `EW.UnregisterEvent`, a new counterpart to `EW.RegisterEvent`, sets `EW.lastRegisterAttempt` around the client call, and `NamesRegisterEvent` now checks for both spellings, so a refusal there turns off only the one capability that event feeds.
- **The probe's tally counts every event exactly once**, allowed or refused, whether or not its own cleanup call is refused too.
- **Loot with combatLog off.** On a client without the combat log, such as Forever, this recorder's kill counter never runs, so a kill that drops nothing is never recorded there; a kill that drops something is unaffected, because it is read off the loot window's own source guid rather than off anything the combat log tracked. Separately, a loot window whose source guid cannot be placed at all, on any client, no longer loses its items: the observation is still recorded, with its subject and its payload's `source_type` marked `"unknown"` rather than the items being thrown away.
- **The idle auction recorder says so.** Where `auction` is off, Auction.lua registers no listener and instead announces once, at the next `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD`, that auction recording is off for the client rather than staying silently idle.

**0.2.1** answers what running 0.2.0 in World of Warcraft: Forever actually showed, pasted back by the owner as `/ewow status`: `game retail` and `client retail (interface 16001, build 1.60.1)`, with `last forbidden action: ADDON_ACTION_FORBIDDEN for EverythingWoWFrame:RegisterEvent() on build 1.60.1 (client retail)` and every one of the three capabilities off.

- **Forever detected off the version string first, whatever the project id.** That paste proved 0.2.0's own assumption wrong: Forever's `WOW_PROJECT_ID` is the mainline id, the same one Retail reports, not the Classic id 0.2.0 expected it to share. 0.2.0 checked the project id before the version string, so a build 1.60.1 session carrying the mainline id read as Retail and switched every capability back on, which is what actually let `RegisterEvent()` reach the client and get refused. 0.2.1 checks the version string, major 1 and minor 60 or higher, before `WOW_PROJECT_ID` is even asked, so this exact session now reads as `forever` and every capability starts off. An unreadable version string still reads as `unknown`, the same restrictive row. `/ewow status` now prints `game <client key>` on its first line rather than the upload's own version key, so a person reads the client this addon actually found rather than the separate key described below.
- **A RegisterEvent refusal is attributed to the event, not to nothing.** The reported function, `EverythingWoWFrame:RegisterEvent()`, is the frame method every recorder's registration goes through, so it was never one of the specific calls `FUNCTION_CAPABILITY` could place, and 0.2.0's handler read that as a report it could not place at all and turned every capability off. `EW.RegisterEvent` now records the event it is mid call on, and a refusal naming `RegisterEvent` is looked up against which capability that event feeds instead, so only that capability turns off. An event that feeds no gated capability, or a report this still cannot place, keeps 0.2.0's fallback: every capability off for the session.
- **`/ewow probe`.** Attempts every event a recorder in this addon registers, one at a time through the same attribution path, undoing each attempt immediately, and prints one line per event: allowed or refused. The result is also written to the saved file under `db.probe`, so an upload carries it once it exists, since the payload schema already tolerates a field it does not read. This replaces waiting for gameplay to trip each restricted call in turn.
- **`/ewow cap <name> on|off`.** Flips one capability for the session, so once the probe has said which events the client allows, the tooltip hook or the GUID path can be turned on and tested on its own without waiting for a code change.
- **The upload's version key is unchanged, and that is now a documented asymmetry rather than an assumed one.** The site's `versions` table has no enabled row for `forever` yet, so `EW.VersionKey`, the key written to `db.version`, still has no `forever` branch and still sends whatever it sent before: `retail` for a session carrying the mainline project id, which is what the owner's own Forever session actually carries. `db.client`, a new field beside `db.version`, carries this addon's own corrected client key instead, `forever` for that same session, and rides along because `readCompanionFile` in the site's `read.ts` reads specific keys off the saved table and ignores any others rather than rejecting them. `/ewow status` reads `db.client` for its `game` line for the same reason.

**0.2.0** answers the World of Warcraft: Forever compatibility block reported on 18 September 2026: an alert reading "EverythingWoW has been blocked from an action only available to the Blizzard UI. You can disable this addon and reload the UI," on build 1.60.1, naming no function.

- **A capability table per client.** The addon now reads `EW.Client` from `WOW_PROJECT_ID`, the interface number, and the version string as soon as `Core.lua` loads, rather than assuming every call works until it errors. Forever cannot be told apart from Classic Era by project id alone, since it is expected to answer with the same one, so the client key is read off the version string's major and minor instead: 1.14 and 1.15 read as Classic Era, 1.60 and up read as Forever. A client the table cannot place reads as Forever too, the most restrictive row rather than the most permissive one.
- **Three capabilities gated, not merely wrapped.** The world cursor tooltip hook (`C_TooltipInfo.GetWorldCursor`, installed inside Blizzard's own tooltip handler), the GUID reader that identifies an NPC or an object, and the combat log listener each sit behind a named capability, and every one of the three defaults off on Forever until an owner's paste proves it safe. Where a capability is off, the restricted call is never made at all: the tooltip hook is never installed, the combat log listener is never registered, and the GUID reader returns nothing rather than pattern matching or comparing what a secret value system may not allow either. The existing `pcall` guards stay in place, because they still catch a changed signature; they were never going to catch this alert, and they still are not what stops it.
- **Forbidden action reporting.** `ADDON_ACTION_FORBIDDEN` and `ADDON_ACTION_BLOCKED` are now handled when either names this addon: the reported function (or, as on the owner's own Forever alert, the absence of one), the client, and a timestamp are written into the saved file, and the matching capability, or every capability where no function is named, is turned off for the rest of the session. `/ewow status` now prints the client, the capability table, and the last forbidden action recorded.
- **No table of contents change yet.** Both `## Interface` numbers are unchanged, and no third table of contents file is added. The suffix a Forever client reads is still unproven, and a guessed interface number is exactly the kind of guess this release is against.

**0.1.1** fixes what the first live test on a Retail client showed.

- **Vendor prices.** Retail no longer has the `GetMerchantItemInfo` global; the merchant frame answers through `C_MerchantFrame.GetItemInfo` instead. 0.1.0 read only the global, so on Retail every vendor item was written with its id and no price at all. Both shapes are read now, and an item bought with a currency records that currency's id and amount beside a copper price of zero rather than looking free.
- **One snapshot a session.** 0.1.0 took its login snapshot on a fixed ten second timer, before the client had loaded the currency list, and could take a second one minutes later for no gain. The login snapshot now waits until the client answers with gear and currencies, and after that a snapshot is written only on `/ewow snapshot`, or after an hour when the gear or the level has changed.
- **Honest counters.** 0.1.0 counted everything it did not write as `skipped`, which reported 802 losses in a few minutes when almost nothing had been lost. `/ewow status` now separates `skipped` (a subject the addon could not place, the only real loss) from `deduped` (a sighting the five minute window already holds) and `ignored` (another player, a pet, a vehicle, a tooltip that is not a world object), and prints each one by reason.

**0.1.0** was the first release.
